## Design standards the owner wants future work to respect

Exploration should reward curiosity through short, understandable experiences. Mechanics, inhabitants, scenery and player actions should support the story. Avoid long compulsory reading, unnecessary detours, chores or friction unrelated to enjoyable play. The Drowned Keeper already supplies optional extended reference material; side locations should add something that gameplay or the existing Archive leaves insufficiently expressed. Review each place individually rather than assigning all five the same puzzle, clear objective or reward pattern.

Functioning institutions and believable residents could better show a living kingdom, including its useful services and ordinary life. Do not reduce Dark Crown to a generic oppressive boss map. These preferences guide future evaluation; the final decision above postpones actual redesign.

### Goblin coherence

The owner pointed out that the Goblin companion archer already demonstrates that Goblins are not inherently evil. Neutral Goblin households alongside aggressive outdoor Goblins need a coherent explanation. Allegiance, occupation and conduct could distinguish civilians from particular guards/patrols who oppose the hero; equipment and behavior could communicate that distinction. This was a proposed solution, not an implemented faction/hostility overhaul. No new bandit faction was established; the owner wants species and hostility to make sense together.

### Flooding continuity

Existing canon leaves the Archive flood's cause unknown. The Keeper's earlier drowning is a separate event. Lantern Wraiths help the Keeper and resent the flooding; the discussion does not make them its cause. Neri's ability to preserve/repair damaged books does not establish that the flooding itself has been solved.

The owner asked whether solving the mystery could allow the Archive to be repaired and unlock a secret. A Watchhouse–Archive investigation, visible restoration and a newly accessible room were explored as possibilities. No culprit, mechanism, quest, room or reward was approved. Preserve the main story's accessibility if such optional content is later designed.

## Magic as a possible future thread

The owner noticed that the Mage hero is the only clearly identified Mage in the visible cast, and wondered who else is mage-adjacent, how magic works, who practises it, what their role is and whether something happened to mages. Staff-like equipment alone does not establish someone's class.

Existing anchors identified during the discussion:

- Mage hero: explicitly the Mage class.
- Lifebinder: binds the fallen into aware, enduring skeletons and practises supernatural arts; not established as the hero's Mage class.
- Drowned Keeper: water magic, drowned echoes and scholarly work; not established as the hero's Mage class.
- Lantern Wraiths: spectral abilities and an existing connection to studying/helping in the Archive; not automatically a society of trained mages.
- Instructors teach the Mage hero's progression, but their personal magical identities/traditions remain insufficiently explained.
- Existing approved lore says Lifebinder and Keeper were former Barracks companions from different, still-undiscovered companion classes. This does not authorize new recruitable classes.

The discussion explored everyday magical knowledge, its transmission and its uses in functioning places. Preservation, shrine rituals, specialised foundry work and signalling were assistant examples, not canon additions. Mage disappearance, persecution, magical guilds, a shared origin for undead beings, or a single magical cause behind all five locations were not established. **The owner subsequently clarified that time may reveal the right use; a magic storyline is not a committed plan.**

## Possible relationship to SIBDL

The owner noted the resemblance between the game title and SIBDL and expects players may theorize about a connection. Their current idea is that the game could be a popular fairy tale/fantasy story told **inside the SIBDL universe**. Its adventures would be fiction within that universe, not a record of actual historical events there. SIBDL canon could eventually supply ideas or answers for the game's worldbuilding.

Treat this as a proposed framing, not finalized shared canon. The existence of such a cultural work could be distinguished from the historical truth of its events. No shared timeline, character identities, historical explanation, public reveal, new SIBDL lore or cross-universe mechanics were selected. Cultural echoes and the kind of fantasy SIBDL inhabitants might imagine remain possible sources of later ideas.
