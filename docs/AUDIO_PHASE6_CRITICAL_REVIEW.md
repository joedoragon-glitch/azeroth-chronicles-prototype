## Source audit and corrective composition choices

**Title — `title`, *The Unfinished Road*, 16 bars at 92 BPM (~41.74 seconds):** Its original new main-story hook is D5–F5–A5–E5: a rising minor-third/major-third gesture with an unsettled falling fourth, a human question rather than royal proclamation. Full-form flute has individually scored later replies and silences; muted piano/harp and carefully located bowed counterlines form a warm-road orchestration. Four sections travel from an open inquiry through widened horizons and interrupted arrival to a returning question. The last exposed E remains an added ninth over D minor. **Strong-opening audit correction:** original accompaniment frames produced too many unsupported early melody arrivals. The revised score intentionally reharmonizes seven title bars (not the protected new hook) and checks every actual accompaniment MIDI pitch-class against the authored chord, leaving meaningful later suspensions. This corrects a real source problem rather than adding another adjective to a score brief. **Unheard risk:** flute/harp may sound too similar to Greenwood's cozy sound; sustained main menu loops could become tiresome; last E can feel either wistful or indecisive.

**Defeat — `defeat`, *The Road Remains*, 8 bars at 76 BPM (~25.26 seconds):** Low cello opens with D–F, a quiet interval echo of the title's first ascending minor third, then loses footing in shorter questions separated by near-empty bars. Piano upper answers and small harp marks stop it becoming punitive triumph or universal permanent death, which the actual Normal/Nightmare/Succession rules do not warrant. The penultimate A-minor descent interrupts the resolution; a last early E over D minor settles to low D. **Strong-opening correction:** chords at bars 1, 2, 5 and 6 were deliberately revoiced to agree with the melody rather than retain accidental clashes. Exactly one early E-over-D suspension remains documented. **Unheard risk:** very slow recorded cello can sound strained or sentimental, and the entire defeat cue may be long for a short UI event; Phase 7 must check the actual event's hold and transition semantics.

**Finale — `finale`, *A Road Still Open*, 16 bars at 80 BPM (48 seconds):** The title's D–F–A–E question is **not pasted at the end**. A4 precedes the D5–F♯5–A5–E5 transformation, with opening notes spread across two measures instead of the title's one-bar climb. The sharpened third offers relief, while B minor, A minor, a borrowed C-natural sonority and an unclosed E over D prevent unearned coronation music. Bowed parts broaden the scene without inventing Dark Crown's unapproved political outcome, score, orchestra or Dark Lord theme. **Strong-opening correction:** new recorded-source harmonic frames were revoiced in five bars, and an otherwise mismatched A-minor piano pitch in bar 7 was caught and corrected by a second independent all-accompaniment audit. **Unheard risk:** a brighter third could imply complete victory if the instrumental balance is over-grand; new voicing may feel too close to the title, or too different for an audience to recognize.

**Mechanical/music-literacy warning:** Counting consonant lead onsets is only a focused error detector. Good suspension, modal mixture, expressive chord extensions and an unresolved ninth may be artistically correct; automated note-on-chord percentages are not an aesthetic grade. The recorded sampler must actually receive **the intended full MIDI stream**, not a score-derived proxy. Phase 5's failures proved that inherited melody notes could be correct while chord frames, register folding or historical accompaniment were wrong. Phase 6 inspects full scores plus sampler-boundary notes, permits no hidden octave changes, and insists every new written note is within four semitones of a pinned source recording.
