# Critical companion survival — dev.141

Joel requested critically wounded companions stop fighting, avoid enemies and keep following him so he can continue an encounter with his healthier companions. This extends the existing party judgment, travel and retreat collision owners.

At 20% HP or below, an active living companion enters individual survival mode immediately. It remains until reaching 40% HP. It does not acquire offensive targets, use active attacks or defensive counterattacks, or finish a pending offensive charge. Critical damage before charge resolution cancels release even when the hit was too small for the normal interruption rule. Forced enemy holds/scatter still take priority until released.

Survival takes priority over build, upgrade, gather and deposit work. Paid progress, orders and cargo are preserved and resume after recovery. Healthy companions keep their own combat/work behavior and the hero's order is untouched. No whole-party Recall is issued. Low-health units are excluded from the fighting support estimate.

The wounded companion selects lower-pressure positions around the moving hero and itself, using nearby enemy awareness, ranged reach and boss special reach. It avoids idle hostiles too. Normal following uses a 420-unit search band; long-range threats expand that band to seek safer spacing. Bounded route planning goes around terrain while avoiding hostile awareness. A blocked destination permits only physically legal local escape steps that improve its safety score. Friendly passage from dev.140 remains; enemies and terrain never grant a retreat escape allowance. Following uses the existing distance-based catch-up pace and movement modifiers. Moving retreat keeps the existing 50% defense, ending on arrival. There is no new healing, invulnerability, speed bonus, teleport, reduced recovery cost or guarantee of survival inside an enclosed or overwhelmingly dangerous arena.

One optional boolean, `survivalRetreat`, retains the recovery threshold through Recall, travel and save/reload, including partial healing above 20%. It is validated in the compatible v4 save schema. Old saves need no migration and critically wounded old-save companions recognize their health on update. Destinations, paths and threat observations remain transient. Recovery to 40% releases this hold; ordinary non-critical caution still applies.

An English/Spanish notice explains that critical companions are avoiding combat, keep following and rejoin at 40%. Existing Ranger healing and receiving existing supportive effects remain available. Attack-linked pet healing still belongs to its offensive skill; survival does not create a free standalone cast.

## Critical-retreat CPU budget — dev.215 published, 29 October 2026 UTC

The 20%-HP critical-survival entry rule, actual live unit health, tactical survival, physical terrain, and v4 saves remain authoritative. The older dev.141 recovery-lock and no-active-attack statements above are **historical implementation notes**; later approved native judgment allows recovery at >20% and situational safe basic contributions. See [critical safe contributions](CRITICAL_SAFE_CONTRIBUTIONS_DEV143.md) and the maintained [development state](DEVELOPMENT_STATE.md) for that later design. This section records a new **performance boundary**, not a reversion of those mechanics.

The owner requested that critically wounded companions cannot consume unlimited terrain checks while hunting the mathematically best escape. The published dev.215 implementation wraps only synchronous critical-retreat destination selection and the stalled local-escape scoring loop with a shared allowance of **200 actual `blocked()` calls per simulation tick**, a maximum **100 per search**, and a **0.25 game-second retry delay after exhaustion**. Nested hero/ally screening, `clearSegment`, and score calculation spend from the same allowance. Planning fails closed: only a fully collision-checked, scored candidate may be used; otherwise the companion holds its current location and retries after the cooldown. Ordinary movement, collision, line-of-sight, other companion actions, and existing route-planning limits are **not bypassed or falsely declared clear**.

Budgets live in weak-keyed, transient in-memory state, reset per simulation time and zone, never appear in v4 campaign snapshots and do not alter previous backups. The budget is a performance and AI-fallibility choice; it is **not an automatic invulnerability, teleport, fixed FPS guarantee, or proof that retreat can always escape a hard enclosure**.

Evidence: [issue #534](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/issues/534), [diagnostic draft #577](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/577), and the focused `tests/crowd-diagnostics.test.cjs` plus `tests/critical-companion-survival.test.cjs`. The test-only unbounded native March reproduction previously recorded 5,284 `blocked()` calls across ten critical-combat updates (single `companionSurvivalPoint` invocation: 3,162). The bounded budget passed the final-head 23-file focused suite ([run 40809478028](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/actions/runs/40809478028)) and was merged in [PR #579](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/579) at [main@bfeb3afb](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/commit/bfeb3afb1cceede6d25332fcf607d841047bac59). [Main Pages run 40809957575](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/actions/runs/40809957575) published the exact build. The same controlled native scene recorded **2,222 vs 5,284** blocked calls, a **~58% reduction**. No full-game/manual browser matrix or physical Chromebook FPS and installed-client update are certified.

Critical followers whose hero is outside the existing maximum tactical advance distance use native collision-safe hero guidance first rather than repeatedly picking local escape destinations. This intentionally preserves established catch-up behavior without granting collision bypass or a distance teleport.
