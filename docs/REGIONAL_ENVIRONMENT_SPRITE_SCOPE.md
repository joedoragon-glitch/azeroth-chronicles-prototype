# Natural scenery production scope · 8 October 2026

Joel explicitly included trees, bushes, flowers, rocks and smaller flora in the sprite reconciliation. The earlier five-representative-only restriction is superseded. Current procedural species, silhouettes, palettes and regional identity remain canon.

The body catalog includes five existing large regional representatives (106–110), 24 newly promoted smaller nature families (271–294), and two newly catalogued field-rock materials (295–296). Exact initial processing contracts are prepared for those 26 additions. The smaller families include dry/wet grass, ordinary/marsh bushes, wildflowers, saplings, stumps, logs, reeds, cattails, driftwood, mangroves, pine saplings, alpine scrub, heather, small rock clusters, dead trees, charred stumps, dry scrub, burned logs, black rocks, crystal clusters, dead shrubs and obsidian. Current authored families, not guessed additional plants, drive production.

Keep all seeded map placement, density, deterministic selection, collision and native apparent size. Capture current seed variations before generation. Produce additional variant members only when their current source identity or repetition QA warrants them; each is its own isolated reference/hash/job. Do not invent new branches, flowers or species to manufacture diversity. Record shared region bindings explicitly, retain small edge/microflora where raster filtering would erase it, and use mixed procedural fallback during integration.

The current generic wild keys distinguish rock from foliage but do not select an image bank per visual seed. Broad tree/rock activation therefore needs variant-bank support before publication. One image repeated across every tree must not flatten the present canopy jitter, flower/moss accents or regional rock treatment. Mirroring can reverse asymmetric canon and is not an automatic variation tool. Small family keys already exist, but secondary region registrations also require exact reference checks. This scope adds no runtime lookup or terrain image.

Water edges, ash patches, ember-pit/fire details, fumaroles and repeated dock geometry stay procedural. They can coexist with richer natural cutouts and the separately catalogued terrain material textures. The first environment comparison should place a large tree, a bush, a flower/grass clump and a small rock in actual day/night scenes to test readability, apparent scale, filtering, repetition and phone/Chromebook frame/memory cost before bulk integration.

Evidence: current natureThemes and authored scenery/harbor dressing in rules.js/world.js; wildProp and decoration branches in visuals.js; exact keys in sprites.js. The machine-readable current family list is tools/sprites/environment-scope.json; broader coverage is GRAPHICS_CANON_SPRITE_COVERAGE.md.
