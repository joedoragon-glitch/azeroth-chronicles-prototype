# Timed Slow now reduces skill cooldown recovery — review candidate

**Owner request:** An enemy Rogue bind, snare, captain/boss Slow, projectile or trap Slow must protect a monster against player and companion ability spam, not merely impair foot movement. Apply the same rule to slowed monsters for predictable status semantics.

- `R.cooldownBalance.slowRecoveryMultiplier = 0.65` for heroes/companions; `enemySlowRecoveryMultiplier = 0.85` for monsters: every second that overlaps actual Slow counts as **0.65 / 0.85 seconds**, respectively, toward remaining skill cooldowns. Sustained Slow makes a cooldown take about **54% longer** to refill, but finite Slow only adds the missing recovery while active. A 3-second Slow adds up to 1.05 seconds of recovery debt to a longer cooldown.
- A skill activated **after** Slow begins starts its full normal cooldown, then immediately recovers at the impaired rate; both pre-existing and freshly activated cooldowns are treated alike. The smaller 15% enemy penalty limits near-permanent Mage frost from suppressing boss attacks too strongly.
- Keep **base cooldowns and talent reductions** intact. Countdown recovery slows temporarily; **do not extend the current timer on every Slow hit**, stack additive cooldown penalties or retroactively modify completed cooldowns.
- Hero Skills 1–8, charged variants, companion basic/global/first/second skill timers, companion Ranger healing/recovery and survival cooldowns, enemy basic/special/summon/recovery timers, captain overtime recruitment, Cindermaw protection, Dreadmaw feeding, Royal Prerogative interception and pending Rogue reaction deadlines all obey this. Status, phase, hazard, cast/warning and animation timers remain at wall-clock speed.
- Slow is still only applied after a valid hit to living nonimmune units; longer reapplications refresh the remaining duration without multiplying penalties. Mage frost now also slows monster skill recovery; this is a reciprocal balance consideration to evaluate against stronger hero skills in draft PR #305.
- Absolute encounter-clock deadlines (currently Rogue reactions and Royal Prerogative) must be adjusted using the **remaining time at the start of each frame**, not only when the deadline is still in the future at the end of a frame. This prevents a near-ready skill from bypassing Slow on its final frame.
- No new persisted field or migration. Save v4 already holds `unit.slow` and remaining cooldowns. Timers recover normally at Slow expiry, including an exact sub-frame expiry.
- Keep the new feature isolated from PR #305 and avoid merge/deploy until an integrated balance review with the completed hero progression overhaul, full automated checks, old-save and phone/desktop gameplay verification.

## Source-only balance assessment (15 October 2026)

**Keep the present provisional 65% allied / 85% hostile cooldown recovery until combined playtesting**, rather than further amplifying Slow before the Skill 3–4 redesign is integrated. A 3-second Slow overlaps up to 1.05 seconds of *lost recovery progress* for heroes/companions and up to 0.45 seconds for monsters. It does not add a flat amount per hit or apply to cooldown time remaining after the debuff ends. Sustained Slow can make uninterrupted hero cooldowns 1/0.65 = 1.54 times longer and enemy cooldowns 1/0.85 = 1.18 times longer. Ordinary movement Slow and the stronger PR #308 Rogue movement Slow remain unchanged.

**Risk 1 — Mage control saturation.** Existing Mage frost can maintain Slow nearly continuously. Proposed rank-8 charged Frost Burst would extend the effect to 5 seconds, while PR #305 also proposes Spell Interrupt (8-second normal cooldown) and a 2–3-second bilateral Silence Field. At the integrated release, inspect cast availability rather than only theoretical attack damage: Slow affects enemy recovery, Silence blocks *new* skills while it applies, Interrupt cancels *one in-progress* warning. Do not secretly extend remaining cooldowns again on Silence or Interrupt; do not stop generic melee/basics unless their authored ability contract requires it. Review normal, TRUE and Awakening bosses with Mage alone and full-party support before any enemy cooldown penalty increase.

**Risk 2 — burst pressure on companions and melee heroes.** A 2.0–2.7s enemy Rogue Slow costs approximately 0.70–0.945s of allied cooldown progress if it persists for its full duration. The slowdown should meaningfully delay newly started heals/defenses and attacks but not stop them completely. Reapplication uses the longest remaining timed Slow, not duration addition, so repeated overlapping snare hits cannot grow indefinite numerical cooldown debt.

**Risk 3 — healing and future passives.** New enemy `supportHealCd` from #306/#308 needs reduced recovery once and only once, while actual heal-over-time pulses and defense buff durations remain at normal wall-clock speed. Carefully decide whether newly introduced reactive passive timestamps represent *ability readiness* (candidate for Slow) or non-skill combat scripting (leave unchanged); preserve their authored proc/phase and expiration semantics. No blanket time multiplier.

**Risk 4 — visibility and regressions.** Current remaining-cooldown readouts should reflect the slowed clock, but the final combined HUD should visibly explain **Slowed: movement and skill recovery reduced**, including accessibility/phone layout. Verify no failed hit, immunity, ordinary knockback without Slow, expired Slow or peaceful-area effect produces a hidden cooldown penalty. Record actual fight durations, successful enemy special casts, hero/companion deaths, healing activations and ability availability in the combined release balance review, without asserting unmeasured success now.
