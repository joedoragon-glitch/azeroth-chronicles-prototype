**Scope:** Phase 1 only, existing 30 SIBDLP recorded-effect identities. Owner explicitly delegated non-listening creative judgment, waived a preliminary by-ear ballot, and asked for real in-game sound replacements with partial/full rollback. ## Source identity and role

The source-led original 30 masters were produced by `tools/audio/render-sfx.py`: 24 material/stage combinations sharing one general spectral-noise-decaying-pitch recipe and six more specialized signals. The `tools/audio/sfx-phase1-master.py` replacement gives 12 distinct material behavior families with release/impact differences and six expressly authored special effects. Its wet suction, dry bone crack, arrow string/shaft, stone mass, steel edge, root fiber, spectral contour, high energy arcane, luminous holy, airborne ash/dust and claw tear have **different transient sources, spectral focus, resonator modes or motion envelopes**, not just one rendered click transposed 24 times. Stage-wise hero and enemy event mapping is retained exactly: 30 stable manifest IDs, eight nonempty recording-bound event keys out of 95; no improper new impact on a miss, no new warning on ordinary actions. The authored Rogue single-bell staff is still procedurally sounded, **not wrongly replaced by an unconnected hero WAV**. Rogue differentiation belongs to later Phase 2's actual-event review.

All 30 effects are original project-owned, 48-kHz 16-bit mono short one-shots. No field recording, CC0 sample borrowing, commercial sound library or AI generative-audio output is falsely claimed. Physical WAV and manifest IDs are explicitly verified by the current CI test. The legacy `sfx-book` generator skips replacements because `managedBy` points to the new renderer, while the original WAV bytes and metadata remain independently frozen.

## Critical first-pass failure and correction — not a cosmetic change

The first 30-master integration was **not** artistically approved simply because its SHA/codec tests passed. Comparing actual first-pass rendered RMS against the 30 original recorded levels revealed a **specific unreviewed mastering problem**: the first hero sword swing reached **3.92×** the old RMS, sword impact **2.15×**, warning **1.77×**, and many other material effects were 1.3–1.7× higher. The common release/impact amplitude targets were effectively flattening original level relationships even when the creative brief did not require louder effects. This directly conflicts with `docs/AUDIO_SOUND_PASS.md` and `docs/AUDIO_AUTHORING.md`'s distinction between sound identity and brute-force loudness.

The revised source uses each old recording's **individual measured RMS** from preserved `tools/audio/sfx-measurements.json` to set a conservative 1.04–1.12× *maximum target* before peak headroom constraints; unlike a uniform compressor it retains the original per-cue hierarchy and allows smaller output when necessary for safe peaks. The `tests/audio-sfx-phase1-quality.test.py` regression gate independently measures **physical 16-bit WAV sample RMS** against those original references and rejects ratios outside 0.80–1.14×. The corrected **30 exact recorded WAVs** on the integration branch have actual measured ratios **0.8958–1.1200×**, with warning **1.04×** and hero swing **1.10×**; sample peak range **0.09859–0.40000**, new RMS range **0.01486–0.03987**. These measured values are source/audio signal facts, **not a human subjective loudness comparison, digital LUFS mastering certificate or proof of good sound on phones**.

The first ten-song-style **experimental sound-effect studies** from the separate pilot branch were not silently copied into the 30 live IDs. That early batch had a low-frequency Ranger impact and excess Rogue bell ring identified and redesigned. The current 30-ID release branch is a deliberately separate, complete replacement suite with stable event identities and no additional waveforms unregistered as new game events.
