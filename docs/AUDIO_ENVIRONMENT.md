## Creative direction

Fourteen original mono 24-second beds previously covered the nineteen places; all remain available, while four new 72-second physical-source beds are now selected for four draft scene routes. They are restrained textures rather than a wildlife show: woodland leaf air (day/night), shallow marsh water (day/night), mountain wind, dry frontier air, sheltered Crown courtyards, quiet homes, secluded stone retreat, damp archive, distant mine/foundry works, vaulted Abyss air, Citadel machinery and a timber cellar. Side interiors reuse suitable beds at lower gains. Bosses reduce ambience to 25% of the exploration gain, combat to 45%, with a short attack and slow recovery. Existing menu/profile/mute controls apply through the ambience bus.

**The original 14 environmental beds** contain no third-party recordings, music or model-generated audio. The **four additional draft recordings** intentionally contain licensed CC0/public-domain physical field/object recordings with verified source author and hash provenance. `tools/audio/environment-book.json` holds deterministic seeds, spectral bands, levels, sparse transient timing and reusable generation briefs. `render-environment.py` supplies periodic spectral synthesis, wrapped physical-style transients and encoding. Creative guides describe the intended result; they are not historical model prompts. The listening room exposes both guides and exact recipe references.

Environmental details remain separate first-match `ambience.*` events with synthesized recovery. They occur 18–36 seconds apart and are suppressed in combat, boss encounters, title and game-over scenes. **Preserve original nocturnal behavior, including on draft [PR #711](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/711):** Greenwood Vale, Ironroot Highlands, recovering Ashen Frontier and Dark Crown outdoors all retain the existing sparse synthesized `ambience.insects` event and its three-tone fallback when no recording is registered. Flooded Marches retains its distinct water detail and additional night tone; Vale daytime retains its sparse bird detail. A previous #709 experiment removed insects from three regions *without owner authorization*; that intervention was explicitly reversed in source, regression tests and offline build fingerprint. **This is working existing environmental content, not an approved claim about the ecology of every region.** Its generic/repetitive character is logged as a future sound-design improvement, but it must remain audible until a scene-appropriate replacement is actually authored, reviewed, approved and integrated. No new animal roars, dense bird choruses, crowds, weather events or continuous insect swarms were added. The current broad sound system does not implement source-positioned waterfalls, individual nearby objects or distance/occlusion simulation.

## Continuing environmental sound-quality standard — findings from #709 (31 October 2026)

**Standing creative authority:** Improving a sound and *removing* an existing sound are different decisions. Preserve the current game recording, procedural detail, day/night alternative and its supported gameplay behavior unless a **specific** replacement is produced and the owner authorizes the change. A finding such as “generic insects,” “two-layer noise,” “overly bass-heavy,” or “Treasuries all share one interior bed” creates a **documented improvement opportunity**; it is **not** approval to silence it, expand animal/weather families, invent new cultures, or substitute a less-audible file. The current owner-approved deliverable is to reconcile **only genuinely produced audio** with the registered game sound. New sounds are not required merely to fill a checklist. The soundtrack is a separate scope; no environmental work may rewrite the Crown score or the soundtrack Phase 7/8/9 listening and integration decisions.

### What this production series established

- **Keep one factual inventory.** Start with [all 14 original MP3 masters and their 19 actual scene routes](AUDIO_ENVIRONMENT_PRODUCTION_INVENTORY_20261031.md), including two different nighttime beds (Vale and Marches), four Treasuries sharing a bed, and side-interior reuse. Separate the *original 14 registered* recordings from the *two superseded interior trials* and *four newer actual 72-second recorded-source candidates*. For every reviewed/new cue, record original ID, scene, source method, exact output filename/hash/bytes, rights, intended replacement ID, playable registration, build status, checks run, artistic hearing status and acceptance. A production candidate stored only in a GitHub Actions ZIP **does not play in the game**.
- **Physical source provenance matters.** The Highland autumn-leaf field recording, Frontier open-field wind and the two sparse object recordings were obtained from real externally documented original files with original authors, public-domain/CC0 evidence, exact byte sizes and independently checked SHA-1s in [regional source ledger](../tools/audio/environment-regional-studies.json). Keep exact source provenance for imported replacements in `credits`; do not relabel them as solely original in-house synthesis. Reject downloads gated by incompatible noncommercial licenses, doubtful provenance, unsuitable weather/noise, or implausible contemporary/microphone content. A licensed source can still be acoustically or narratively wrong.
- **Compose a specific physical place, not a label.** A believable autumn hardwood highland has real leaf/air variation, but Stonecross industry is not proved by the word “Highlands”; a recovering Frontier cannot sound permanently on fire; a working Ogre signal station should not simply receive the outdoor ridge wind nor a loud casket creak; an inhabited Orc barracks must not be confused with a vacant shrine or modern cutlery. Distinguish outdoor enclosure, sheltered field-air leakage, building materials, occupation and near/far depth. Avoid invented voices, horn ritual, fauna, heavy machinery or weather as a shortcut. The [19-place canon/creative risk ledger in this guide](#709-soundtrack-grade-soundcraft-audit-and-corrective-studio-31-october-2026) is the starting location-by-location reference.
- **Real field recordings must materially shape the texture.** The old fourteen-bed renderer uses exactly two procedural broadband noise layers; seven original beds lack discrete authored details. Earlier studio pilots proved that one short object creak over synthetic noise does not establish a lived-in environment. The later physically recorded field studies used ~72.53% original field source in Ironroot outdoors, 56.04% in Frontier outdoors, and—after rejecting synthetic-dominant first interior mixes—27.61%/31.39% in Signal Keep/Shrine. These are signal-energy measurements, **not** perceptual authenticity scores; never set universal creative mix ratios just to game a test.
- **Prevent obvious looping and sonic fatigue.** The early 24-second prototypes would place the same one-shot at the same instant every loop, so they were superseded by independently authored 72-second beds, not three repetitions of a 24-second file. Actual MP3 codec guards, full decoded loop endpoints, first/last one-second envelopes and 24-second block correlation must be inspected. Correlation below 0.021 excludes the naïve triplication defect; it does **not** establish how a 10-minute session sounds. The final Shrine one-second envelope differs by ~**4.011 dB** at its seam: a near-threshold engineering result deliberately retained as an **audible-risk HOLD** rather than falsely called seamless. A testing tolerance is not a substitute for hearing.
- **Correct acoustic defects at their source.** The Highland raw recording's unusually high isolated peak (~0.502 sampled linear full scale, 24.31 dB one-second crest) was bounded with a smooth-knee adjustment in the authored preview, producing a decoded peak near 0.234 / 17.76 dB crest, without changing the global ambience/music bus. The first new interior field-air mixes carried only ~7.73% / 15.40% real field energy; they were rebalanced individually to ~27.61% / 31.39%. Another first-generation physical one-shot was buried ~21–24 dB beneath its own bed; correcting that local event did **not** justify globally boosting all warnings or environments. These measured improvements still need ear-based checking for audible compression artifacts, excessive one-shot recognizability and musical masking.
- **Measure what can be measured, and label it honestly.** Check physical file byte identities, codec/sample rate/channel count, integrated LUFS (loudness units relative to full scale), true peak (maximum reconstructed signal), RMS (signal energy), sampled spectra, clipping, source contribution, transition and memory cost. Analyze the **encoded and decoded** master, not only pre-encode arrays. A waveform can technically pass and still sound lifeless or intrusive. The original score often dominates the bed by roughly 20–33 dB at defaults in source-level scenario estimates; the measured post-compressor Foundry gap was ~24.95 dB desktop / 27.29 dB phone-*emulated*. The new outdoor files are slightly *quieter* than their original source references. Do not claim these files are clearly audible on speakers, preserve combat warning intelligibility, or already work in a full soundtrack mix without a real integrated check.
- **Respect actual device and packaging budgets.** Four 72-second mono 32-kHz float32 decoded buffers would require ~36.86 MB together, in a game whose default audio cache targets 96 MiB; the ~4.63-MB compressed candidates are not the whole memory footprint. Measure actual stream/buffer loading, at most one outgoing transition, cancellation, combat/boss attenuation, optimization/mute, phone-emulated browser behavior, warning priority and offline update, using current *registered* files. The field recording source WAVs (more than 195 MB together) were temporary studio inputs, not shipped media. Do not silently alter loop bounds, overwrite generated `audio-library.js`, or omit the worker fingerprint update.
- **Test integrity is part of creative responsibility.** An early workflow reported green while hiding the actual preview MP3s from artifacts; this was repaired with explicit hidden-file inclusion and a **fail-on-missing-artifact** gate. Normal focused CI initially lacked FFmpeg/FFprobe; the audit installed real decoders rather than skipping acoustic tests. The next studio workflow was narrowed so large original recordings were not pointlessly re-downloaded for duplicate PR/push events. Old strict tests assume **14 original 24-second source-book masters**: when authentic recordings enter the manifest, deliberately update those contracts and exercise the new actual files. Do **not** remove assertions, use diagnostic mocks as proof of recorded fidelity, or equate a green CI check with artistic acceptance.
- **Preserve what is already working pending later improvements.** The original sparse `ambience.insects` night fallback is **retained** in Vale/Highlands/Frontier/Crown and the Marches remains water-oriented. Highlands/Frontier/Crown genericity is a **pending creative review**, with replacement sound **not authored**. The original sound is not a blocker solely because we have identified a better future design direction. The same preservation rule applies to all 19 locations that lack a new source-authored candidate.

## Author or replace a bed

`assets/audio/manifest.json` remains the runtime authoring source. `director.environment` contains:

```json
{
  "combatGain": 0.45,
  "bossGain": 0.25,
  "detailGap": [18, 36],
  "rules": [
    {"id": "archive", "when": {"zone": "archive"}, "asset": "env-archive", "gain": 0.33, "fade": 2.5}
  ]
}
```

Rules use the existing scene match fields, first match wins. Put exact interiors and night variants ahead of broad region matches. Gains range from 0 to 1; fades from 0.025 to 4 seconds. Referenced assets must have `kind: "ambience"` and valid loop bounds. The shared contract validates registration, build and playback before changing live sources. Removing the optional environment section restores the original background texture for older catalogs.

For an original texture adjustment, edit its book entry and run:

```sh
python3 tools/audio/render-environment.py --id env-archive
```

The renderer changes only assets marked `managedBy: "environment-book"`, preserves custom imported replacements, edited guides and manifest routing, and never changes music assets. Renderer output uses 150 ms periodic codec guards; the loop spans 0.15–24.15 seconds. New book entries also need explicit catalog routing. Obsolete recordings are removed deliberately after their routing has been changed.

For an external replacement, keep the existing ID and register the approved local recording with measured bounds:

```sh
npm run audio:register -- env-archive assets/audio/ambience/my-archive.mp3 --kind ambience --loop-start 0.15 --loop-end 24.15 --author "Joel" --license "CC0-1.0"
```

Bounds are illustrative for a new file. Registration marks the replacement as imported and removes renderer ownership. The original renderer then preserves it. Adding a new ID works the same way; point an environmental rule at it. No runtime code changes are required to replace a sound or route a new location.

## Lifecycle and release

Rapid scene changes cancel stale starts; a departing bed fades out even while the next file loads. Active and outgoing buffers use the shared pinned decoded LRU cache (96 MiB default under the 31 October 2026 PR #712 budget revision), source limits and cleanup. Pause freezes the context and resumes the same active loop. Catalog replacement stops old sources and reloads the same scene; invalid replacement leaves playback untouched. Failed loads use the original synthesized background without retrying every frame. Environmental diagnostics are separate from music errors in `recordingStatus()`.

The 14 beds contributed approximately 3.9 MiB in the original release. **Historical original baseline:** 127 catalog assets (83 music, 14 ambience, 30 effects), approximately 24.5 MiB of encoded audio. **PR #711 source-integrated candidate:** 131 assets (83 music, 18 ambience, 30 effects), adding 4,633,524 bytes of physical MP3s while preserving the former 14. The 256 MiB source-package limit still applies; the 96 MiB decoded cache is separate. Chromium desktop/phone-emulated traversal passed the memory gate (maximum observed 84.80 MiB / 96 MiB) but was not a physical-iPhone test. Decoding stays lazy. All registered files join the versioned offline cache. Desktop and phone use the same environmental owner; the phone profile reduces ambience and retains mono compatibility. The listening room can audition individual beds and full contextual scenes without loading app/persistence or touching campaign saves. Replaying an audition replaces its previous recording instead of stacking loops.

Release follows `AUDIO_AUTHORING.md`: bump version, regenerate entries/service worker, run catalog/format/build checks, regression tests and desktop/phone audio/offline browser checks before publishing.
