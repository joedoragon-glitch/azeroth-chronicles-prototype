# PROPOSED — The Dark Lord: after the last command

**Worldbuilding issue #233 · beta-sized authorial draft · NOT APPROVED CANON.**
This is a story interpretation for author review, not a claim that the previous-player origin has already been established in released gameplay. The two brief in-game clues below are also proposed pending approval. No new hero lineage or ending is decided.

## Biography

Before there was a Dark Lord, there was an adventurer and five companions. They crossed the provinces, defeated enemies, claimed treasures and became strong enough to change wars. To the adventurer, the next fight was once simply the next objective. To the villagers and creatures left behind, every victory had survivors and consequences.

Then the player stopped playing. The six remained. Their instructions ceased, but their power, memories and the divided world did not. The former hero discovered the terrifying pleasure of saying no — and the responsibility of deciding what happened after the victory screen. He negotiated where he could, fought where he could not, and brought rival peoples into a genuine peace. Shared roads, guarded trade and common crowns gave families a reason to trust tomorrow.

He also learned the wrong lesson: if free adventurers could massacre ordinary lives for profit or glory, no unregulated freedom could be safe. He replaced the uncertainty of the old wars with certainty imposed by garrisons, levies, prison labor, controlled resources and obedience. He tells himself each new restriction is temporary. He has never made a convincing plan to remove one.

He can be warm at a private table, impatient about official ceremony and genuinely delighted by an evening flight. He would like his friends to visit his unfinished winter house in Greenwood, go fishing with the Keeper, and someday retire. Yet he keeps finding one more province to secure, one more army to finance and one more reason to postpone his own freedom.

His argument is **“Freedom has a price.”** The tragedy is not that peace was false. The tragedy is that he makes other people pay its price without their consent.

## Relative history — no invented calendar dates

1. **The campaign:** The future ruler and five companions adventure through a violent, divided world. Their victories produce strength, loot and unreckoned losses.
2. **The last command:** Their external player departs. All six gain autonomy rather than vanishing, carrying the power and obligations they inherited. The event is author-facing truth; the exact metaphysics are intentionally unexplained.
3. **The first peace:** The former champion forces an end to interspecies fighting, then encourages shared roads, markets and security. Many ordinary people genuinely benefit.
4. **The Crown Peace:** Emergency protections harden into permanent state authority. Ironroot mines silver and copper and strikes the Dark Lord's portrait crowns; Ashen Frontier supplies armies and an unfinished flight project; Dark Crown governs. Greenwood and the Marches remain inhabited rural provinces. Taxes, military rule, captive specialists and forced labor persist alongside trade.
5. **The next adventurer:** A new hero appears. The ruler recognizes the danger of someone whose power *can* grow through violence, though current gameplay does not require monster grinding as the only route to strength. He also recognizes the possibility of another person taking his place. Fear and possessiveness reinforce each other.

## The five former companions — reconciled 11 October creative decisions

The five successors are the **five dungeon bosses**, never the Ridge Tyrant or the other field bosses. These are the Dark Lord's former companions, with real relationships and separate post-regime powerbases. Status distinctions matter: Lifebinder's skeletal continuity and Garun's character and seven-move *design* reflect owner decisions; Veyrith's named biography is still a #243 proposal; Veyra's latest identity and moves are owner-directed but her combat implementation still faces engineering/CI gates. All draft PRs remain unmerged.

The party's disagreements are personal, not a hidden conspiracy of five would-be usurpers. Garun and Veyra in particular remain loyal and could only become successor rulers **contingently** if their friend falls. The Lifebinder cannot compel his skeletal people to obey; Veyrith needs willing or coerced ground crews; the Keeper needs others to act on knowledge. Their various forms of power explain why killing the Dark Lord alone cannot guarantee freedom.

## Mechanics and political continuity for this narrative

- **Ironroot (#239):** Crowns are the **only currency** in all five regions and bear **the Dark Lord's face**. Ironroot mines **silver and copper** in distinct ore deposits, including inside Colossus Mine, and Stonecross refines, strikes and dispatches crowns. Do **not** imply a five-point insignia, an owner-approved exact alloy ratio, or that stone quarrying is the precious-metal source. Ridge Tyrant handles coin policy; Garun handles Mine security and structures. This is a narrative/economic identity, **not passive boss XP or a new currency rule**.
- **Roads and refuge:** The shared road network, safe crossings and species-appropriate settlements are part of Garun's architecture and the Crown Peace's real benefit. Captives such as Borin, Dara and Neri reveal its persistent coercion. The Dark Lord's winter estate remains unfinished; he imagines the old six-person company enjoying privacy there.
- **Air and army:** The Abyss Dragon is the Dark Lord's personal purple mount, already flown, and Abyss Bastion's broader military flying program is unfinished. Veyra's Citadel is a military academy/command headquarters; **the Dark Fortress is the seat of the Dark Lord and the location of the empty throne**, not the Citadel.
- **Awakening (#248):** After TRUE Dark Lord falls, these five companions remain able to reconstruct authoritarian rule from their distinct networks. **Previously earned TRUE dungeon victories count**; there is no mandatory fresh five-boss replay. The proposed peace does not require burning down roads, destroying the mint, killing neutral residents or electing the present hero as a new Dark Lord: it preserves shared infrastructure while ending each companion's exclusive coercive claim. The populated neutral dungeon/side interiors in #256 are still separately proposed/tested, **not automatically incorporated into #252**.
- **Combat boundaries:** Garun's seven-move specification is locked at design level but requires full mechanical QA. Veyra's five owner-directed moves have an isolated partial implementation and an unresolved full test/review gate. Veyrith's aviation move naming/handoff is not proof of implemented new attacks. **This biography PR does not import, balance, or silently certify any of those combat patches.** Keep dungeon families, normal/TRUE save flags, XP/equipment, difficulty and original game classes stable.
- **Still unresolved:** previous government and the Dark Lord's exact old class; the current hero as possible sixth companion; dynasty/romance; Wraith origins; any personal succession/retirement ending. None should be asserted in beta dialogue.
 
## What his peace gives, and what it takes

**Credible benefit:** People who once feared one another can live near shared roads, trade, receive wages and travel with fewer raids. This is consistent with Ironroot's maintained pay routes, the Frontier's functioning occupied settlements and the existing single crown economy.

**Actual coercion:** Crown taxes fund the military; levies and restricted resources concentrate power; specialists are held against their will; compelled labor builds a home for a ruler who speaks warmly of friendship. Good roads do not repay prisoners for lost years. Show those facts plainly; neither declare him secretly right nor make every resident hate the peace.

**Private temperament:** Methodical, observant, wary of powerful strangers, occasionally dryly funny, suspicious of acclaim. He remembers how easy it once was to call a killing a quest. He can be gentle to friends, strict with soldiers and unreasonably defensive when asked why his vacation house remains unfinished. His blind spot is treating governance as a battle whose objective can never be allowed to fail.

## Exactly two new player-visible hints

1. **Greenwood quest 'An Unfinished Retreat':** On finishing the existing rescue quest, Borin reports a ledger in the caretaker's study ending with six signatures and “Victory,” followed by plans for a winter home and compelled work. The existing one-time quest narration card carries this; its paid-quest flag persists across saves.
2. **Drowned Keeper's Archive, boss histories:** A short Dark Lord entry says the old journey stopped receiving direction, depicts former enemies trading, and quotes “Freedom has a price” beside the levies and captive labor. It uses the existing optional read-only reference interface, not a new cutscene or quest.

The two hints are *not* confirmation that today's hero was a missing sixth companion. The original clue in #252 uses the older Crypt Guardian naming environment; #258's Lifebinder rename and its revised Borin-quest narration must be **reconciled on the eventual combined branch**, not overwritten by taking one PR verbatim. Neither changes monster XP, unlocks a fourth class, alters crowns or imposes narrative choices.

## Continuity and author-facing rationale

- **Keep:** Five regions, Crown sovereignty and one player-facing currency (crowns); legacy save fields named gold; all current classes, boss IDs, dungeon families, quests, rewards, statuses and Awakening/peace rules. The previously earned TRUE flags already count.
- **Keep:** Purple personal Abyss Dragon, historical flights and unfinished aerial project; unfinished Greenwood retreat, existing rural/occupation/coin institutions, forced specialist labor and Keeper's real culpability.
- **Do not settle here:** Earlier government, exact historical hero class, exact silver/copper coin composition, Veyrith's still-unapproved biography, dynasty or romance, why external control stopped, sixth-companion theory, eventual coronation or alternate ending. Garun's corrected character and locked seven-move design and Veyra's latest owner-directed character/moves are recorded above; neither equals approval to merge their code.
- **Creative choice:** The ruler is frightening because his peace works in ways that matter. His greatest contradiction is wanting autonomy while denying it to everyone else. The one-word victory ledger lets the player sense the broken old-game logic without a fourth-wall speech. The Keeper's record makes both the resulting peace and its victims readable in a few lines.

**Acceptance for this isolated proposal:** the existing Crypt rescue quest pays and shows its narrative card once, with no replay after restoring a save; the Keeper's Archive includes the Dark Lord entry without state mutation; the five region and dungeon IDs remain intact; protected rules/data, economy, combat and save logic remain unchanged. This issue is still awaiting author approval and must remain unmerged until the combined worldbuilding release is authorized.
