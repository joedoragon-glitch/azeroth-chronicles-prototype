## Architecture and budgets

Small sprite accessories, auras and attachments execute their **unchanged canonical drawing recipes** through a GPU-path recorder. Path nodes and gradient resources are retained and released with their actor; static signatures skip rebuilding. Dynamic aura geometry changes without generating body textures. Accessory paths apply the original asset overlay scale and the body's foot-anchor facing. Independent entity aura and registered rank regalia remain distinct original layers and each is painted once.

The original Canvas `visualPositionFor` contract now positions **both** registered and procedural actor artwork at the live idle/busy presentation point. Registered accessory regalia, silhouette clipping and boss attachments follow that body point. Shadows, ground rings, target marks, world interactions, collision and saved actor coordinates remain at the unshifted world foot anchor. A real-composite stationary-hero browser fixture compares Canvas and WebGL2 at two presentation times while asserting no simulation mutation or fresh body textures; the native parity suite guards the distinct body-versus-ground coordinate inputs. This specifically resolves the final idle-bob gap discovered during the Work-mode review without animating or rebaking whole textures.\n\nWildlife is separated **once during preparation**: one static home texture and one texture per authored animal. The original painter's optional separation mode suppresses other paint operations while extracting each animal. Tests reconstruct the original composition for every family at two times. Steady frames move/flip these cached animals using `ambientAnimalPose`; no clock is added to the body-cache key and no per-frame raster uploads are introduced. All parts count toward the existing byte budget and normal eviction lifecycle.

Occlusion reuses the existing four-buffer alpha compositor, capped by the original 24-actor/16-cover eligibility limits. It reads resident CPU images corresponding to the GPU's exact selected frame/crop/pivot/facing. It neither reads GPU pixels nor uploads mask textures. The initial 1/8/24/48 crowd probe retained **four 256² surfaces, 1,048,576 bytes**, with **zero additional body bakes** during measured frames. Software-GPU JavaScript timing is diagnostic, not physical-device FPS; final paired timing evidence is recorded in the acceptance receipt.

Fields and victim marks deliberately live below actor bodies; Choking Ash's original full-Canvas late composition remains unchanged. This corrects world-depth semantics in Pixi instead of painting a screen-wide warning over the characters. Night grading therefore also applies to the GPU ground field. Partial ellipse paths use subpixel native polylines; antialiasing differences do not establish missing artwork or universal pixel identity.

## Reproduction and acceptance

`tests/pixi-parity-browser.test.cjs` serves the real ordered runtime source graph, creates one deterministic Campaign and uses controlled authored visual fixtures without running combat simulation. It prepares actual Pixi WebGL2 and captures **page composition**, including the transparent Canvas cue layer. It fails on GPU preparation/fallback, unexpected saved-state mutation, missing repaired layer ownership, frozen wildlife, stale expired cues or time-driven texture growth. Native combat/Archive suites separately own attack and eligibility mechanics; the visual fixtures do not claim to exercise those mechanics through player input.

Presentation times are explicit, camera/state/viewport/DPR/zoom are held constant, and both renderer captures use the same Campaign object. Source/engine/settings, resource counters, frame timings, images and region-of-interest motion counts are retained in `test-artifacts/parity-748*/`.
