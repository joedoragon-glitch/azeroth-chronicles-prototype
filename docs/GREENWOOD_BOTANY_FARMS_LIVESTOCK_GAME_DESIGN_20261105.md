## The problem: landscape structure, not a shortage of trees

The region currently has credible large hardwood silhouettes, but the ground layer and livelihood layer are weak. Small plants are disproportionately associated with canopy stands; open meadows and traveled country read as untextured grass. A few illustrated flowers recur while field plots are so small that they resemble resource tokens. Several authored props are visually isolated or concealed by trees. The `Orchard expansion` nameplate/marker competes with a road, despite the orchard's intended agricultural purpose. More randomly distributed props would reproduce this problem at higher density.

The governing creative direction is the approved [50-decision Greenwood register](GREENWOOD_VALE_OWNER_ART_DIRECTION_DECISIONS_20261028.md): mature-summer, green eastern-Piedmont-like fantasy countryside; oak/hickory-like trees; a broad cultivated valley; layered woodland; managed banks beside farms; dense woodland understory but sparse road margins; distinct Millhaven and quieter Orchard Hamlet; organic fruit planting, several growth stages, mixed fields/pasture/kitchen gardens; clear roads, services and 150% camera readability. This is not a new real-world species catalogue, new gathering profession, historical plantation aesthetic or permission to modify canon and quests. [Flagship #545](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/issues/545) remains relevant, as do [livelihood #548](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/issues/548) and [the concurrent road/bridge design PR #807](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/807). Road geometry, junctions and bridge widths should be reconciled with #807 *before* freezing permanent plant setbacks. Do not merge or cherry-pick #807 casually.

## Visual design contract: five coherent botanical layers

**1. Sunlit ground / open country.** Restore recognizable dandelion puffs, tiny cream/yellow/lavender/pink wildflower blooms, mixed grasses and a few low herbal rosettes in *irregular*, modest patches. Use several small clusters in open meadows and farm-to-forest transitions, with deliberately quieter intervening grass. Do not use one evenly tiled repeated stamp or create a carpet of confetti. Each 150%-camera open-country composition should have some readable ground variety without forcing a flower into every road/encounter view.

**2. Woodland edge.** Mixed bushes, low broadleaf thickets, young plants and occasional flowers belong just outside mature crowns as well as beneath them. Denser understory remains in interior woodland; edge plants taper outward. Planting should not visually merge into an impenetrable wall or conceal trees' trunks, combat actors or paths.

**3. Domestic margins.** Small wildflower beds, verge grasses and modest bushes belong beside **home sides/back yards**, garden boundaries, fences and maintained farm lanes. Keep the working street, service entrances, refuge and shops free. A few carefully chosen domestic groupings are more convincing than the same patch repeated beside every building.

**4. Agricultural land.** Distinguish a compact green **kitchen garden** (near a house), a larger tilled **field** (multiple visible furrows, coherent soil footprint), a fruit **orchard** (widely spaced mature and young trees, organic mixed-age groups), and **pasture** (open grass, animals, feed and modest fencing). Fields must relate spatially to homes, storage and farm access. A lonely ploughed diamond beside a workshop is not acceptable rural land use.

**5. Water margins.** Reeds and low wetland plants grow on the actual dry/wet bank transition, not in water centers or as a swamp coating. Drier flower and grass patches can appear beyond the managed edge. Greenwood should not become Flooded Marches. The eastern pond is a practical farm-water feature; any nearby livestock needs a believable accessible path and paddock.

### Distribution, scale and recognizable scenery

- Keep the current mature hardwood trees; **do not** increase tree count merely to fill vacant ground. Distribute small plants across open land, edges and pathsides as the botanical hierarchy requires.
- Reuse approved procedural `wildflowers`, `bush`, low grasses, `understory`, `fruit-tree`, `sapling`, `reeds` and dandelion ground language first. Any new artwork remains canon-faithful, distinct enough at real gameplay scale and subject to the existing asset validation contract.
- Establish a few low-density meadow corridors between Millhaven, Hamlet and woods; richer mixed-height forest margins; tidy kitchen plots near cottages; and orchard-scale productive blocks behind the Hamlet. Darker enemy territories should remain ecologically plausible without cheerful ornamental flowers interfering with danger cues.
- The existing garden/furrow icon should **not** simply be globally doubled for every region and save. Create Greenwood-specific multi-bed visual compositions: roughly **2–3 current icons in width for a modest kitchen plot**, **3–5 icons' visual footprint for small farm fields**, then verify actual scale, open gaps and crop row perspective on a 150% phone capture. These are composition bands, not final hardcoded collision radii.
- Orchard target: enough added fruit trees in **several separated, mixed-age groups** that the Hamlet visibly functions as an orchard community, including trees beyond the named expansion. Start with an 8–12-*additional-tree placement budget* for visual experiments, then accept, reduce or redistribute on actual camera screenshots and route/collision evidence. Do not pack them all around one marker or hide the living area under crowns.
- Cultivated land should have **visible area** (several beds/rows), **visual ownership** (near cottages or farm plots), **access** (lane/farm gate without drawing over the rows), and **support** (tools/storage appropriate to that parcel). Keep plots out of the road and away from unrelated isolated workshops. Existing stable prop IDs should be preserved where visual relocation can be done lawfully.
