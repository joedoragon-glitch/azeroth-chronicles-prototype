# Curated artistic source and sample-palette workflow

**Standing creative-production practice — adopted 31 October 2026 by the project owner.**
Applies when making or commissioning **SIBDLP music, sound, world/environment art, interiors, characters, interface graphics and related visual assets**. The same method is a reusable preference for other artistic commissions: choose meaningful reference material and palettes, preserve provenance, compose deliberately, and critically review the actual result. This is a quality-production method, **not permission to reuse proprietary artwork or publish anything**.

## Why this exists

The owner values a thoughtful selection of **sample banks, sample libraries, reference sets, material palettes and reusable stylistic vocabularies**, used as ingredients rather than as one-click substitutes for authorship. The older soundtrack Phase 3 was wrongly described as compositionally ready when distinct source-file schemas and clean MP3 encodes had not proved musical identity, believable instrumentation, or genuine listening quality. See [soundtrack master issue #673](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/issues/673) and [draft correction PR #704](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/704). Do **not** repeat that source-to-acceptance error in any art discipline.

## Repeatable production method

1. **Determine the artistic purpose first.** Read existing game canon, authorial preferences, scene purpose, camera/viewing distance, accessibility needs, runtime constraints and any intended preserved assets. A village, dungeon, spell, character and combat warning have different jobs. State what must feel familiar and what must change.
2. **Research and curate a source palette.** Compare suitable licensed sound libraries, instrument banks, procedural components, texture/brush sets, architectural and natural references, color/material palettes or typography. Reject poor-resolution, inconsistent, mismatched, low-quality or provenance-uncertain inputs. A sample palette should be small, coherent and useful—not a random bulk download.
3. **Keep a provenance ledger before importing.** For every reusable input record creator, origin URL, pinned upstream version/commit, exact filenames or content hashes, license, license evidence, attribution obligations and transformation notes. Check both *source material* and the library's own redistribution terms. CC0 is appropriate for unrestricted reuse, but it does not make the resulting work artistically excellent. For visual references, use original observation or permission/licensed assets rather than copying another artist's protected composition.
4. **Establish an intentional design system.** Document what the palette conveys and where it belongs. For music: melodic identity, harmonic language, instrument register, articulation, dynamics, timing, spatial placement and interaction with gameplay. For environments/interiors: silhouette, focal point, believable materials, lighting, scale, negative space, traversal and camera composition. **No interchangeable generic preset** may masquerade as a scene-specific treatment.
5. **Make a scoped representative prototype and inspect what it actually produces.** Save source alongside representative rendered outputs. A named horn patch might sound like an oscillator; a procedural tree may have a poor silhouette; a beautiful texture might tile awkwardly. Test the resulting picture or sound rather than approving the authoring recipe by name.
6. **Conduct an adversarial quality review before scaling.** Compare the old and new identity, examine at least one contrasting scene to catch sameness, flag artifacts, musical register/voice-leading or visual composition problems, evaluate repeated exposure and reject weak outputs. Verify compliance with technical budgets separately (build, cache, audio decodes, frame rate, memory, file sizes, accessibility). **Successful automation cannot certify an artistic impression.**
7. **Record a truthful gate and handoff.** For each category distinguish *source created*, *rendered*, *technically tested*, *independently heard/seen*, *artistically accepted*, *merged*, *published* and *device verified*. A missing viewing or listening step is **UNVERIFIED**, never assumed green. A demonstrated issue is **REVISE/HOLD**, and derivative production must not multiply it. Owner authorial acceptance remains with the owner at the scheduled phase.

## Soundtrack Phase 3 — current example, not approval

- **Scope:** eight regional/dungeon base themes in [draft #704](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/704); no Phase 4 expansion; 83 existing registered music IDs, 65 current replacement candidates, and all 18 frozen Dark Crown tracks remain protected.
- **Source-composition repair:** [actual performed-vs-original motif review](../tools/audio/phase3-quality-review.json), checked by [a regression test](../tests/audio-phase3-quality-review.test.cjs). Six new opening melodies were corrected to restore historical pitch/onset contours. Two recycled Phase 2 bases (Greenwood and Archive) still require a critical treatment. A matching note pattern does not prove good harmony, form or timbre.
- **Bank integrity:** [`tests/audio-phase3-sample-bank.test.cjs`](../tests/audio-phase3-sample-bank.test.cjs) checks all 55 copied MP3/Ogg binary Git hashes, sizes, source metadata, instrument coverage gaps and the explicit isolated sampler integration boundary. Passing this means **provenance only**, not natural sound quality or enough recorded pitches.
- **Recorded-instrument palette:** [phase3-sample-bank.json](../tools/audio/phase3-sample-bank.json) now indexes **55 pinned CC0 recordings across ten instrumental palettes**, from tiny-orchestra's VSCO-2 chamber instruments plus Meta Open Sound's recorded **piano and nylon guitar**. Original credits, specific license evidence, pinned commits and individual Git blob SHA-1s are in [the bank's README](../tools/audio/phase3-samples/README.md). The [isolated sampler](../tools/audio/phase3-sampler.py) is **implemented on draft PR #704** and the [dedicated isolated studio test](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/actions/workflows/audio-phase3-studio.yml) is validating it. **The bank is NOT registered in the game, no candidate recordings have been accepted by hearing, and Phase 4 remains BLOCKED.**
- **Constraints:** recorded instruments still require sensible register, dynamics, overlap/voice allocation and good arranging. The chamber bank includes already-lossy compact 96-kbit/s mono MP3s, whereas the piano/guitar clips use Ogg Vorbis; repitching, repeated short sustain segments and hard sample switches can sound artificial. Validate original Git blob hashes and sample decoding before building on them. Consider higher-quality source material if the sonic results are inadequate.
- **Gate:** Phase 3 **SOURCE MUSICAL QUALITY REVISE; rendered technical evidence for the *revised* eight-score candidate must be separately established; GENUINELY HEARD SOUND UNVERIFIED; PHASE 4 BLOCKED.** Any former green label was withdrawn. This quality restriction is independent of the [live dev.242 package-budget increase in PR #712](https://github.com/joedoragon-glitch/azeroth-chronicles-prototype/pull/712). No owner first audition is expected before Phase 9.
- **Engineering debt:** because PR #704 is a stacked draft based on a pre-#712 main state, integration with the newer main and an unrelated sprite-canon evidence failure must be resolved carefully. Do not silence a sprite test in an audio PR or call mixed focused CI green merely because music checks pass.

## What a satisfactory evidence record contains

For each deliverable: identity/location/goal; approved visual or sonic references; deliberately selected sample/material palette; exact versions and licenses; design decisions and intentional deviations; first-pass and corrected source; hashes and links to actual outputs; independent critical findings and resolved issues; technical measurements and test-run SHA; whether an actual human heard or saw the artifact; cross-scene contrast review; merge/publication/device status; owner acceptance boundary.

**Stop rule:** Don't advance to the next production phase because the schedule says so or because all files exist. Advance only after the *specific required gate* passes without concealing open artistic concerns. Preserve findings where the next developer can locate them in GitHub, not just in chat.
