### 1. One cohesive vision

Greenwood is a **mature-summer working river valley**, not a grass canvas full of collectible-looking tokens. Its readable scene sequence is: welcoming Millhaven freight crossroads → smaller domestic kitchen gardens and farm transitions → productive Orchard Hamlet with mixed-age fruit groves, fields, smallholding yards and a working cellar → wet millbank and service lanes → deeper varied woodland → southern timber footbridge and less cultivated eastern land. The north stone bridge carries freight; the south timber bridge carries people/pack travel. Both remain real routes. Some open land must remain open for exploration and combat. The eastern pond is managed farm water, not a swamp.

Art direction: rounded oak/hickory-like hardwood crowns, medium saturated summer greens, warm timber and soft earth; occasional muted pink, cream, lavender and pale yellow flowers, real-looking white dandelion seed heads, darker shaded thickets. Clay wear appears selectively on cart tracks and exposed banks. Farm plots are connected land-use shapes, not standalone tiny geometric icons. No modern signs, asphalt, ornamental flowerbeds everywhere, plantation architecture, extra fruit species without canon check, or new harvest/loot economy.

**Player-facing success:** At a glance and without reading a map label, a traveler can recognize *which way carts go*, *where people grow food*, *why sheep are standing there*, *which crossing is for freight*, and *where wild countryside begins*. This must hold at the native 150% landscape-phone camera, not only in a decorative overview.

### 2. Final design hierarchy (priority order in overlapping spaces)

1. **Protected gameplay / movement:** existing river and legal crossings, walls and gates, NPC service fronts, hero and companion travel, combat telegraphs, quest markers, dungeon entrances, Barracks/owned structures, finite resource nodes, saved identities. Never visually imply a usable surface the movement solver blocks.
2. **Real cart/farm/foot route network:** topologically connected centerlines, turns, junctions, bridge and banks; roadbed/shoulders with honest visual clearance. Road geometry precedes fixing adjacent parcels.
3. **Working/inhabited parcels:** market, residential yards, kitchen gardens, field blocks, fruit groves, cellar/loading chain and livestock pens; linked to a genuine approach, not scattered to fill voids.
4. **Structural vegetation:** retain mature woodland; arrange shrubs, hedges, young growth and bank plants by soil/land use and canopy edges.
5. **Low ground layer:** grasses, dandelions and wildflower drifts added under a controlled density budget, with selective negative space.

**Conflict rule:** If a candidate flower patch, field edge, fruit tree or animal enclosure intrudes into a proposed road/bridge's complete **swept footprint** (traveled surface + necessary shoulder/turning and pedestrian clearance), move or reshape the *scenery parcel*. If a road proposal conflicts with an existing critical building/service, redesign the route/junction with actual geometry; never sacrifice the service to satisfy a cosmetic curve. A saved/owned building is a protected case requiring a compatible source-aware resolution, not deletion. If neither fits, the route/parcel layout is **not approved**, and engineering must resolve it before visual implementation.

### 4. Farm-plot, orchard and domestic composition

**Millhaven:** Houses and workshops retain distinct functions; civic avenue is kept readable. Compact kitchen gardens occur beside/back of residences with two or three visible beds and a small practical edge/foot access. Farms begin beyond domestic margins, not in market turning space. Mild flowers by quiet walls, poultry in an open enclosed yard, and a few bushes away from services; no sheep hidden under roofs.

**Orchard Hamlet:** The main visual identity is **fruit cultivation dominating more than cottages do**. Create three or more readable **mixed-age fruit groups**: (a) established bearing grove near the Hamlet, (b) young/new planting in the expansion parcel, (c) a smaller peripheral working grove near a legitimate farm route and away from the same single marker. Organic spacing and walkable gaps—not aligned rows or dense canopy enclosure. The 8–12 *additional trees* from #808 is an **initial sketch budget only**, not a required exact count: prove the finished camera composition and avoid overwhelming the road, existing mature hardwoods or households. Do not add new named fruit variants without source/canon review. The current separate working fruit cellar remains decorative/compatible; do not confuse it with the Goblin-occupied Old Orchard Cellars dungeon.

**Cultivated field hierarchy:** 1) **kitchen garden** — 2–3 small beds, close to a residential household, visibly tended; 2) **working field** — connected bank of multiple coherent furrows, visually around 3–5 current plot-icon footprints as a study starting point, with irregular field perimeter and a farm-track access; 3) **young orchard planting** — tree pits/saplings and cultivation soil; distinct from 1 and 2; 4) **pasture** — grazed clearing, fodder/trough, plausible fence/pen where justified. These are **visual composition bands**, not hardcoded physics or universally resized `garden` art in all regions. Avoid a big single enlarged diamond or a crop rectangle floating atop a road.

**The workshop-adjacent furrow problem:** `greenwood-orchard-6` at authored [1130,1870] is plausibly the isolated field near `settlement-minor-3` workshop [1000,1920]; camera-ID match is still a **candidate identification**. Inspect live camera and give the cultivation group a plausible household/hamlet field parcel; keep workshop tools adjacent to the workshop, not as decoration legitimizing a farm that has no farm. Also inspect other `garden` and `furrow-plot` IDs for similarly misplaced plots. Avoid creating an unjustified new farm building/worker mechanic merely to justify the art.

**Production sequence:** visible planted ground → trees or crops → storage/drying/tools/crates → current working fruit cellar → farm lane to Millhaven/transport. This is environmental storytelling using existing decorative objects, **not** an added economy, new interactable or requested species catalog.

### 5. Botany and vegetation density by land-use context

Use the botanical detail in #808 and the five layers below to avoid an even "fill all empty pixels" approach:

| Environment | Ground layer | Mid-height layer | Open-space discipline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Millhaven working street | Very restrained trampled grasses and soil | Rare maintained shrubs at side/rear yards | Keep practical routes, labels, lanterns, doorways and turns uncluttered. |
| Millhaven domestic edges | Small loose flower groupings, selected dandelions | Occasional bush or kitchen-garden foliage | Cluster beside homes, not over entrances. |
| Hamlet orchard/farms | Dandelions in grass margins, sparse wildflowers and planted bed detail | Fruit trees, saplings, low boundary thickets | Keep rows, field edges, walking gaps and labeled orchard marker distinguishable. |
| Open meadow / farm-to-forest | **Noticeable** dandelion puffs and mixed muted flower drifts in irregular islands | Occasional low brambles/shrubs | Alternate enriched patches with stretches of open grass for encounters and rest. |
| Woodland edge and inner stands | Grasses, scattered flowers where light reaches, leaf/earth marks | Layered bushes/thickets and understory under/around crowns | Dense within forest but taper outward; no impassable-looking green hedge. |
| River/pond margin | Sparse damp grasses and accurate shoreline reeds | Occasional low bank vegetation | Keep actual water and wet collision legible; farm banks managed, not marsh-clogged. |
| Goblin/Skeleton/den/estate | Less ornamental growth, rough natural weeds | Contextual thickets outside combat/gate areas | Preserve hostile silhouettes, telegraphs, patrols, camp structures and atmosphere. |

**Dandelion canon:** Dandelion procedural seed heads already exist in `visuals.js::groundDetail` for Greenwood (`k === 4`), but the sample is tiny and one branch among eight, with some tile details skipped. The improvement is **visibility + coverage** across open land, not removal/re-creation of a supposedly deleted asset. Use deterministic static/cacheable scattered filaments and occasional tiny clusters with scale/contrast tuned for real 150% viewing. No per-frame independent animation for hundreds of plants. The existing `wildflowers` prop draws four colored blooms: vary group arrangements and visual sizes carefully rather than stamping identical dark ellipses everywhere.

**Composition rule:** When seen from one camera, a player should notice at least one purposeful transition in groundcover and at least one stretch of breathing room. Floristic density must vary across the region and decrease naturally on trampled, cultivated or dangerous ground. Keep perennial summer identity; no new weather/season systems.

### 7. Composition storyboard (required playable scenes)

Each frame must be checked at *normal gameplay 150% landscape-phone framing*, in addition to at least one regional diagnostic overview. Engineering should produce real-world, same-camera before/after evidence; no invented concept image substitutes for game captures.

1. **Millhaven start/main street:** instantly understandable freight artery with market and accessible fronts; almost no ground clutter in center, modest lived-in verges.
2. **Millhaven residential side lane:** house, small kitchen garden, grass and flowers all logically arranged without blocking a door or roof silhouette.
3. **A genuine T/Y junction:** one clean roadbed and shoulder, orchard/meadow vegetation stopping outside travel; no overlapping color discs.
4. **North freight bridge, both banks in one frame:** stone deck in proportion to cart road, aligned to actual water banks and real traversable corridor.
5. **Hamlet arrival:** the road progressively becomes rural, orchard and useful fields visibly outweigh cottages.
6. **Orchard expansion:** mixed young and mature fruit, planting rows, source marker beside—not on—road, work/storage chain legible.
7. **Workshop/field composition:** clearly separate workshop tool-yard from cultivated household/farm land; plot scale credible and accessible.
8. **Millbank and working approach:** farm path and dry apron reach real mill, reeds exactly at water, no obstructed sluice/tailrace.
9. **Eastern farm pond + livestock:** whole sheep bodies, nearby fodder/trough/pen, dry walking approach, pond still recognizable.
10. **Sunlit meadow passage:** multiple recognizable dandelion heads, sparse pastel flower islands, low thickets and sufficient open ground.
11. **Woodland edge → deep stands:** graded understory rather than sudden tree-only/blank-grass switch; visibility for combat/hero.
12. **Thornfang den:** recognizable sleeping/nest asset and purposeful inhabited subdivisions; no circle hidden under canopy.
13. **Southern timber crossing, both banks:** readable narrower footbridge, road-to-walking transition and actual linked path.
14. **Hostile approaches and estate:** wilderness groundcover moderated around combat/gates; no blocked Skeleton/Goblin routes or telegraphs.
15. **Night comparisons:** same composition, sufficient separation/visibility using the separately designed lighting #809; do not recolor the world as part of this scope.

### 8. Performance and persistence design boundaries

**Actual renderer:** Source owner `greenwood-landscape.js` creates authored layout and `greenwoodRefinementScene` contains a seven-card additive overlay. `world.js` handles region lifecycle/route clearance; `visuals.js` renders road, bridges, props and cached ground, with `renderer.js` and `pixi-experimental.js` composing the scene. Gameplay routing/collision is authoritative outside the painter. Keep one render/simulation state.

**Savings:** Preserve v4 saved campaigns, stable IDs, four existing playable classes and all named NPCs, six Greenwood quests, finite tribute/resource totals, companions/worker orders, paid Barracks, original Goblin/Skeleton defenses, Thornfang/Mira and Scornfang, dungeon entries and both crossings. **Do not rerun the destructive prior `greenwoodRedesignFinish`** just to move plants. Design for versioned, idempotent **additive scene changes** with legal narrow relocation when source ownership allows it, leaving past saves' gameplay state intact. Verify repeated restore does not duplicate props or reproject the map. Rebaseline all existing authored props against new *swept* road corridors, not old road centerline setbacks alone. Real foliage/tree visibility and collision conventions must agree.

**Cost:** Draw street/bridge geometry as bounded *precomputed or cached* static topology, not continuous costly curve intersections. Place small decoration deterministically and lazily/cacheably; prefer finite shared compositions or cached ground to hundreds of new per-frame entities. Respect known 150% phone memory/render time constraints and both Canvas and Pixi; monitor cold/warm scene preparation, texture/cache pressure and sustained iPhone observations. More visual detail **does not** authorize more enemies, economy nodes, live NPC AI, sounds, animations or higher device draw cost by default. Keep unrelated regions untouched.

### 10. Consolidated acceptance and "not acceptable" contract

An implementation may be offered for authorial acceptance only if (1) the **full map** reads as a coherent rural valley; (2) the player can traverse all 16 intended routes and **both bridges** as originally needed; (3) the main cart, farm and foot hierarchy is visible with no double-painted road or fake legal ground; (4) **fields, orchards and livestock** visibly belong to inhabited farms; (5) dandelions, wildflowers, shrubs and layered understory appear in open land as well as near woods, without overplanting; (6) the four specific owner-reported misplaced/hidden assets are independently identified and resolved; (7) camera, road/door/service/actor visibility holds at 150% phone view and both renderer paths; (8) v4 saves/owned objects/quest/loot/combat state remain intact; (9) any performance cost is measured and acceptable rather than assumed; and (10) no unsupported claims are made about tests, merging, publication or devices.

**Must revise:** cartoonish mini crop tiles or uniform flower carpets; a forest canopy hiding livestock, landmark or sleeping prop; orchard expansion marker still on a road; large straight-edged road/bridge panels, hard hairpins or overlapping caps; narrow painted deck with giant invisible legal water crossing; unreachable door/bridge; altered combat cues, saved structures or payouts; or serious unchecked renderer cost.

**Design-only completion receipt:** The **relationship between the two previous design streams is settled** by this contract. Final per-prop coordinate approval, exact road curvature meshes, detailed art output and live phone appearance remain **implementation and perceptual acceptance steps**, not fabricated as completed in the design phase. Engineering can start from L0–L4 without inventing the aesthetic hierarchy or having to reconcile competing designer instructions. No source, asset, save, gameplay or publishing change is part of this document.
